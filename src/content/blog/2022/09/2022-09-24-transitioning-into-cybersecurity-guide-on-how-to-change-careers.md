<p>I’m happy to help provide suggestions or recommendations given my experience and what I’ve seen my mentees achieve. With that, you have the right approach on wanting to improve your fundamentals to get into cybersecurity in general and not just the ACTP. There are two ways to go about this - either get some real life experience or find a good learning path to gain the skills needed. The former is more of a long game and with too many dependencies, which leaves you with the option to study. There are good courses, many free or inexpensive on LinkedIn learning or Coursera. I would suggest though trying for a certification. This way you gain the knowledge and get something to show for it on your resume. </p>



<p>Some good certifications that can start as a basic include CompTIA certifications like A+ and Network+, with which you can then go for the Security+. All of this can be achieved in a few months with dedication and hard work. </p>



<p>Once you’ve got that under your belt, you stand a great chance of breaking into an entry-level Cybersecurity role such as a Triager or SOC Security Analyst. These are a good way to start learning the environment, threat hunting, threat intelligence, and even incident response. </p>



<p>If you’re more interested in offensive cybersecurity then you can look at PenTest+ for penetration testing as a career option (red teaming). This can be fun if you like hands-on hacking. </p>



<p>Tech Support or Product Support is a very logical first step to get into cybersecurity. I started in Technical Support during the early days of my career. However, at least a decade before that, as a 13-year old who was sent for “computer classes” as a way to channel my teenage energy, I ended up inadvertently foraying into Cybersecurity. In this class that used shared computers, I had learnt to set up a phishing webpage that mimicked Rediff Mail (there wasn’t Gmail then) because I was curious about HTML. I ended up setting this as the homepage on all of the machines and pretty soon I had a bunch of username and passwords. My ethical compass never let me do anything with it though and I ended up immediately shutting it down as I was, honestly, quite scared. </p>



<p>Years later, I got into multi factor authentication (MFA) early on learning to create my own authenticator. I ended up dabbling in MFA, VPN, HIPS and a bunch of other security stuff within my first role in Technical Support. </p>



<p>That could be something else you look at - ie. setting up a lab at home and trying your hand at learning a SIEM, penetration testing, or learning to use tools such as Metasploit/John the Ripper/nano, etc. which were some of the tools we learned to use in the ACTP. I can help guide you on where to start if you’d like, but this will require some effort from you. </p>
